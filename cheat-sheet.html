Systems of Linear Equations

KnowExample
Linear equation: $a_1x_1+\cdots+a_nx_n=b$ (no products/roots of variables).
$3x_1-5x_2=-2$ is linear; $4x_1-5x_2=x_1x_2$ and $x_2=2\sqrt{x_1}-6$ are not.
Solution set of a system is always: none, exactly one, or infinitely many. Consistent = at least one; inconsistent = none.
Two lines: cross at a point (one), parallel (none), or coincide (infinitely many).
Augmented matrix $[\,A\ \mathbf b\,]$: coefficients aligned by column, constants last. Size is rows $\times$ columns.
$x_1-2x_2+x_3=0$, $2x_2-8x_3=8$, $5x_1-5x_3=10$ becomes the $3\times4$ matrix
$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&-2&1&0\\0&2&-8&8\\5&0&-5&10\end{array}\right]$$
Elementary row operations: replace ($R_i\leftarrow R_i+cR_j$), interchange, scale by $c\ne0$. Row-equivalent augmented matrices ⇒ same solution set.
$R_3\leftarrow R_3-5R_1$ turns row $[5\ 0\ {-5}\mid10]$ into $[0\ 10\ {-10}\mid10]$; reverse it with $R_3\leftarrow R_3+5R_1$.
Solve: eliminate downward to triangular form, then clear upward from the last pivot. Check in the original equations.
$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&-2&1&0\\0&2&-8&8\\5&0&-5&10\end{array}\right]\sim\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&-2&1&0\\0&1&-4&4\\0&0&1&-1\end{array}\right]\sim\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&0&0&1\\0&1&0&0\\0&0&1&-1\end{array}\right]$
Solution $(1,0,-1)$; check: $1-0-1=0$ ✓
Inconsistent signature: a row $[\,0\ \cdots\ 0\mid b\,]$ with $b\ne0$.
$x_2-4x_3=8$, $2x_1-3x_2+2x_3=1$, $4x_1-8x_2+12x_3=1$ reduces to a row $[0\ 0\ 0\mid15]$: $0=15$, no solution.
Two fundamental questions: existence (any solution?) and uniqueness (only one?). Both readable from triangular form.
$2x_1-x_2=h$, $-6x_1+3x_2=k$: $R_2+3R_1$ gives $[0\ 0\mid k+3h]$ ⇒ consistent iff $k+3h=0$.

Row Reduction and Echelon Forms

KnowExample
Echelon form (REF): zero rows at bottom; each leading entry strictly right of the one above; zeros below leading entries. Reduced (RREF): also leading entries are $1$ and are the only nonzero in their column.
REF $\begin{bmatrix}\blacksquare&*&*&*\\0&\blacksquare&*&*\\0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}$, RREF $\begin{bmatrix}1&0&*&*\\0&1&*&*\\0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}$ ($\blacksquare\ne0$, $*$ anything)
Thm 1The RREF of a matrix is unique. (REFs are not, but their leading entries sit in the same positions.)
Both $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&4\\0&3\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ and $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ are REFs of the same matrix; the only RREF is $I_2$.
Pivot position = spot of a leading $1$ in the RREF; pivot column contains one; a pivot is the nonzero number you actually use there.
$\begin{bmatrix}0&-3&-6&4&9\\-1&-2&-1&3&1\\-2&-3&0&3&-1\\1&4&5&-9&-7\end{bmatrix}\sim\begin{bmatrix}\boxed1&4&5&-9&-7\\0&\boxed2&4&-6&-6\\0&0&0&\boxed{-5}&0\\0&0&0&0&0\end{bmatrix}$
Pivot columns 1, 2, 4.
Algorithm(1) leftmost nonzero column; (2) get a nonzero pivot on top (swap); (3) zero out below; (4) cover the row, repeat on the rest — forward phase → REF. (5) From the rightmost pivot, zero out above and scale to $1$ — backward phase → RREF.
$\begin{bmatrix}0&3&-6&6&4&-5\\3&-7&8&-5&8&9\\3&-9&12&-9&6&15\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}3&-9&12&-9&6&15\\0&2&-4&4&2&-6\\0&0&0&0&1&4\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-2&3&0&-24\\0&1&-2&2&0&-7\\0&0&0&0&1&4\end{bmatrix}$
Basic variables ↔ pivot columns; the rest are free. General solution: basic in terms of free; every variable listed; free ones are the parameters.
$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&0&-5&1\\0&1&1&4\\0&0&0&0\end{array}\right]\Rightarrow\begin{cases}x_1=1+5x_3\\x_2=4-x_3\\x_3\text{ free}\end{cases}$
Thm 2Consistent iff the last column of $[A\ \mathbf b]$ is not a pivot column. Then: unique iff no free variables; infinitely many iff some free variable. Decide this from REF — no need for RREF.
REF $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3&-9&12&-9&6&15\\0&2&-4&4&2&-6\\0&0&0&0&1&4\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: no $0=b$ row, free $x_3,x_4$ ⇒ infinitely many solutions.
WatchFree variables in an inconsistent system mean nothing — the set is empty. And "$x_3=1+x_2$" alongside "$x_2=-1+x_3$" is not a general solution (which one is free?).
$4\times7$ coefficient matrix with 4 pivots: pivot in every row ⇒ consistent for any $\mathbf b$; $7-4=3$ free ⇒ infinitely many.

Vector Equations

KnowExample
$\mathbb R^n$ = column vectors with $n$ real entries; equality, $\mathbf u+\mathbf v$, $c\mathbf u$ entrywise; $\mathbf 0$ = zero vector. $(3,-1)$ means the column $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3\\-1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, not the row $[3\ \ {-1}]$.
$\mathbf u=(1,-2)$, $\mathbf v=(2,-5)$: $4\mathbf u+(-3)\mathbf v=(4,-8)+(-6,15)=(-2,7)$.
Geometry: $\mathbf u+\mathbf v$ = 4th vertex of the parallelogram on $\mathbf 0,\mathbf u,\mathbf v$; $c\mathbf u$ scales length by $|c|$ (flips if $c<0$); all multiples of $\mathbf u\ne\mathbf 0$ form the line through $\mathbf 0$.
$\mathbf u=(3,-1)$: $2\mathbf u=(6,-2)$ same direction, twice as long; $-\tfrac23\mathbf u=(-2,\tfrac23)$ opposite.
Linear combination $c_1\mathbf v_1+\cdots+c_p\mathbf v_p$ with weights $c_i$ (any reals, zeros allowed). Algebraic properties (i)–(viii): commutative, associative, $\mathbf 0$, $-\mathbf u$, two distributive laws, $c(d\mathbf u)=(cd)\mathbf u$, $1\mathbf u=\mathbf u$.
$\mathbf u=3\mathbf v_1-2\mathbf v_2$ means: go 3 steps along $\mathbf v_1$, then 2 steps against $\mathbf v_2$.
Vector equation = system: $x_1\mathbf a_1+\cdots+x_n\mathbf a_n=\mathbf b$ has the same solutions as the augmented matrix $[\,\mathbf a_1\cdots\mathbf a_n\ \mathbf b\,]$. So "$\mathbf b$ is a combination of the $\mathbf a_i$" ⇔ that system is consistent.
$\mathbf a_1=(1,-2,-5),\ \mathbf a_2=(2,5,6),\ \mathbf b=(7,4,-3)$: $\left[\begin{array}{cc|c}1&2&7\\-2&5&4\\-5&6&-3\end{array}\right]\sim\left[\begin{array}{cc|c}1&0&3\\0&1&2\\0&0&0\end{array}\right]$ ⇒ $\mathbf b=3\mathbf a_1+2\mathbf a_2$
Span$\{\mathbf v_1,\dots,\mathbf v_p\}$ = the set of all linear combinations (always contains $\mathbf 0$). $\mathbf b\in$ Span ⇔ $[\,\mathbf v_1\cdots\mathbf v_p\ \mathbf b\,]$ consistent.
$\mathbf a_1=(1,-2,3)$, $\mathbf a_2=(5,-13,-3)$, $\mathbf b=(-3,8,1)$: reduction ends in $[0\ 0\mid-2]$ ⇒ $\mathbf b\notin\operatorname{Span}\{\mathbf a_1,\mathbf a_2\}$.
Picture: Span$\{\mathbf v\}$ ($\mathbf v\ne\mathbf 0$) is a line through $\mathbf 0$; Span$\{\mathbf u,\mathbf v\}$ ($\mathbf v$ not a multiple of $\mathbf u$) is a plane through $\mathbf 0$.
$\mathbf u=(3,1,0)$, $\mathbf v=(1,6,0)$ span the $x_1x_2$-plane.
WatchA free variable does not guarantee consistency.
$\mathbf y=(-4,3,h)$ vs $\mathbf v_1=(1,-1,-2)$, $\mathbf v_2=(5,-4,-7)$, $\mathbf v_3=(-3,1,0)$: last row $[0\ 0\ 0\mid h-5]$ ⇒ in the span iff $h=5$, despite a free variable for every $h$.

The Matrix Equation $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$

KnowExample
Definition: $A\mathbf x=x_1\mathbf a_1+\cdots+x_n\mathbf a_n$ — columns of $A$ weighted by the entries of $\mathbf x$. Needs (#columns of $A$) = (#entries of $\mathbf x$).
$\begin{bmatrix}1&2&-1\\0&-5&3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}4\\3\\7\end{bmatrix}=4\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}+3\begin{bmatrix}2\\-5\end{bmatrix}+7\begin{bmatrix}-1\\3\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}3\\6\end{bmatrix}$
Row–vector rule: entry $i$ of $A\mathbf x$ = (row $i$ of $A$)·$\mathbf x$. $I_n\mathbf x=\mathbf x$.
Same product: $1\cdot4+2\cdot3+(-1)\cdot7=3$; $0\cdot4+(-5)\cdot3+3\cdot7=6$.
Thm 3$A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ ⇔ $x_1\mathbf a_1+\cdots+x_n\mathbf a_n=\mathbf b$ ⇔ system with augmented matrix $[\,\mathbf a_1\cdots\mathbf a_n\ \mathbf b\,]$. Hence $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ is solvable iff $\mathbf b$ is a combination of the columns of $A$.
$x_1+2x_2-x_3=4,\ -5x_2+3x_3=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \begin{bmatrix}1&2&-1\\0&-5&3\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x=\begin{bmatrix}4\\1\end{bmatrix}$
Thm 4For $A$ ($m\times n$) these are equivalent: (a) $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ solvable for every $\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^m$; (b) every $\mathbf b$ is a combination of the columns; (c) the columns span $\mathbb R^m$; (d) pivot in every row of $A$.
$A=\begin{bmatrix}1&3&4\\-4&2&-6\\-3&-2&-7\end{bmatrix}$: $[A\ \mathbf b]\sim\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&3&4&b_1\\0&14&10&b_2+4b_1\\0&0&0&b_1-\frac12b_2+b_3\end{array}\right]$
Zero row in $A$ ⇒ not for every $\mathbf b$; solvable exactly on the plane $b_1-\tfrac12b_2+b_3=0$ (= span of the columns).
WatchThm 4 is about the coefficient matrix. A pivot in every row of $[A\ \mathbf b]$ proves nothing (the pivot may be in the $\mathbf b$ column). Also (d) needs $n\ge m$.
$A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\\0&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $A\mathbf x=(3,2,0)$ solvable, $A\mathbf x=(3,2,1)$ not, though $[A\ \mathbf c]$ has 3 pivots.
Thm 5$A(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)=A\mathbf u+A\mathbf v$ and $A(c\mathbf u)=c(A\mathbf u)$.
$A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&5\\3&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, $\mathbf u=(4,-1)$, $\mathbf v=(-3,5)$: $A(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)=A(1,4)=(22,7)$ $=(3,11)+(19,-4)=A\mathbf u+A\mathbf v$.

Solution Sets of Linear Systems

KnowExample
Homogeneous $A\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$: always has the trivial solution $\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$; has a nontrivial one iff there is a free variable (so always if more unknowns than equations).
$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}3&5&-4&0\\-3&-2&4&0\\6&1&-8&0\end{array}\right]\sim\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&0&-\frac43&0\\0&1&0&0\\0&0&0&0\end{array}\right]\Rightarrow\mathbf x=x_3\begin{bmatrix}4/3\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}$
$x_3$ free ⇒ nontrivial; solution set = line through $\mathbf 0$.
Parametric vector form: $\mathbf x=s\mathbf u+t\mathbf v$ (free variables as parameters, numeric vectors). Homogeneous solution set is always Span$\{\mathbf v_1,\dots,\mathbf v_p\}$: 1 free var → line, 2 → plane, through $\mathbf 0$.
$10x_1-3x_2-2x_3=0\Rightarrow\mathbf x=\begin{bmatrix}.3x_2+.2x_3\\x_2\\x_3\end{bmatrix}=x_2\begin{bmatrix}.3\\1\\0\end{bmatrix}+x_3\begin{bmatrix}.2\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}$
a plane through $\mathbf 0$.
Thm 6If $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ is consistent with a particular solution $\mathbf p$, its solution set is $\{\mathbf p+\mathbf v_h:\ A\mathbf v_h=\mathbf 0\}$ — the homogeneous set translated by $\mathbf p$. (Proof: $A(\mathbf p+\mathbf v_h)=\mathbf b+\mathbf 0$.)
Same $A$, $\mathbf b=(7,-1,-4)$: $\sim\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&0&-\frac43&-1\\0&1&0&2\\0&0&0&0\end{array}\right]\Rightarrow\mathbf x=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}-1\\2\\0\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf p}+t\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}4/3\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf v}$
line through $\mathbf p$ parallel to the homogeneous line.
ProcedureRREF → basic in terms of free → write $\mathbf x$ as a vector → split into (constant) + free$_1\cdot$(vector) + free$_2\cdot$(vector)… Check: $A\mathbf p=\mathbf b$, $A\mathbf v_i=\mathbf 0$.
Planes $x_1+4x_2-5x_3=0$ and $2x_1-x_2+8x_3=9$ meet in the line $\mathbf x=(4,-1,0)+x_3(-3,2,1)$.
WatchThm 6 needs consistency; inconsistent ⇒ empty set, nothing to translate. The direction vectors are the same for $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ and $A\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$; only $\mathbf p$ changes.
$10x_1-3x_2-2x_3=7$: $\mathbf x=(.7,0,0)+x_2(.3,1,0)+x_3(.2,0,1)$ — the plane above, shifted by $(.7,0,0)$.

Linear Independence (1.6 skipped)

KnowExample
Independent: $x_1\mathbf v_1+\cdots+x_p\mathbf v_p=\mathbf 0$ has only the trivial solution. Dependent: some weights not all zero work (a dependence relation). Test: row reduce $[\,\mathbf v_1\cdots\mathbf v_p\ \mathbf 0\,]$; free variable ⇔ dependent.
$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&4&2&0\\2&5&1&0\\3&6&0&0\end{array}\right]\sim\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c}1&0&-2&0\\0&1&1&0\\0&0&0&0\end{array}\right]$
$x_3$ free ⇒ dependent; $x_3=5$ gives $10\mathbf v_1-5\mathbf v_2+5\mathbf v_3=\mathbf 0$.
Columns of $A$ independent ⇔ $A\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ has only the trivial solution ⇔ pivot in every column (no free variables).
$A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1&4\\1&2&-1\\5&8&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\sim\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&2&-1\\0&1&4\\0&0&13\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: 3 pivots ⇒ independent.
By inspection: $\{\mathbf v\}$ independent iff $\mathbf v\ne\mathbf 0$. Two vectors: dependent iff one is a multiple of the other (same line through $\mathbf 0$). This multiple test is for two vectors only.
$(3,1),(6,2)$ dependent ($\mathbf v_2=2\mathbf v_1$); $(3,2),(6,2)$ independent. $(-2,4,6,10),(3,-6,-9,15)$: ratio $-\tfrac32$ fails at the 4th entry ⇒ independent.
Thm 7A set of $\ge2$ vectors is dependent iff some vector is a combination of the others; if $\mathbf v_1\ne\mathbf 0$, some $\mathbf v_j$ is a combination of the preceding $\mathbf v_1,\dots,\mathbf v_{j-1}$.
$\mathbf u=(3,1,0),\mathbf v=(1,6,0)$ independent; $\{\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf w\}$ is dependent iff $\mathbf w$ lies in their plane ($w_3=0$).
Thm 8More vectors than entries ($p>n$ in $\mathbb R^n$) ⇒ dependent. Thm 9Contains $\mathbf 0$ ⇒ dependent.
$(2,1),(4,-1),(-2,2)$ in $\mathbb R^2$: dependent by Thm 8 though no two are multiples. $\{(2,3,5),(0,0,0),(1,1,8)\}$: dependent by Thm 9.
WatchThm 7 does not say every vector is a combination of the others — testing one chosen vector is invalid. Pairwise independence ≠ independence. Thm 8 says nothing when $p\le n$.
$\mathbf u=(3,2,-4)$, $\mathbf v=(-6,1,7)$, $\mathbf w=(0,-5,2)$, $\mathbf z=(3,7,-5)$: every pair independent, $\mathbf w$ is not a combination of $\mathbf u,\mathbf v,\mathbf z$, yet the set is dependent (4 vectors in $\mathbb R^3$).
1.8

Introduction to Linear Transformations

KnowExample
Transformation $T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$: rule $\mathbf x\mapsto T(\mathbf x)$. Domain $\mathbb R^n$, codomain $\mathbb R^m$, image $T(\mathbf x)$, range = all images (⊆ codomain).
$T:\mathbb R^5\to\mathbb R^2$ with $T(\mathbf x)=A\mathbf x$ ⇒ $A$ is $2\times5$ (rows = codomain, columns = domain).
Matrix transformation $\mathbf x\mapsto A\mathbf x$: range = Span of the columns. "Which $\mathbf x$ map to $\mathbf b$?" = solve $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ (uniqueness); "is $\mathbf c$ in the range?" = is $A\mathbf x=\mathbf c$ consistent (existence).
$A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-3\\3&5\\-1&7\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $T(2,-1)=(5,1,-9)$; $T(\mathbf x)=(3,2,-5)$ only for $\mathbf x=(1.5,-.5)$; $(3,2,5)$ is not in the range (row $[0\ 0\mid-35]$).
Linear: (i) $T(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)=T(\mathbf u)+T(\mathbf v)$, (ii) $T(c\mathbf u)=cT(\mathbf u)$. Equivalent single test: $T(c\mathbf u+d\mathbf v)=cT(\mathbf u)+dT(\mathbf v)$. Every matrix transformation is linear (Thm 5).
$T(\mathbf x)=3\mathbf x$: $T(c\mathbf u+d\mathbf v)=3c\mathbf u+3d\mathbf v$ $=cT(\mathbf u)+dT(\mathbf v)$ ✓ (a dilation; $0\le r\le1$ would be a contraction).
Consequences: $T(\mathbf 0)=\mathbf 0$; superposition $T(c_1\mathbf v_1+\cdots+c_p\mathbf v_p)$ $=c_1T(\mathbf v_1)+\cdots+c_pT(\mathbf v_p)$. Quick disproof: $T(\mathbf 0)\ne\mathbf 0$.
$T(x_1,x_2)=(x_1+1,\,x_2)$ has $T(\mathbf 0)=(1,0)\ne\mathbf 0$ ⇒ not linear.
Geometric matrix maps: projection, shear, rotation — each moves the unit square to a recognizable shape; segments map to segments.
Shear $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $(0,2)\mapsto(4,2)$, base fixed. Rotation $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $(4,1)\mapsto(-1,4)$, i.e. $90^\circ$ counterclockwise.

The Matrix of a Linear Transformation

KnowExample
Thm 10Every linear $T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$ is $T(\mathbf x)=A\mathbf x$ for the unique standard matrix $A=[\,T(\mathbf e_1)\ \cdots\ T(\mathbf e_n)\,]$, $\mathbf e_j$ = $j$th column of $I_n$. (Because $\mathbf x=x_1\mathbf e_1+\cdots+x_n\mathbf e_n$.)
$T(\mathbf e_1)=(5,-7,2),\ T(\mathbf e_2)=(-3,8,0)\Rightarrow A=\begin{bmatrix}5&-3\\-7&8\\2&0\end{bmatrix},\ T(\mathbf x)=\begin{bmatrix}5x_1-3x_2\\-7x_1+8x_2\\2x_1\end{bmatrix}$
From a formula: read each output entry as a row.
$T(x_1,x_2)=(3x_1+x_2,$ $5x_1+7x_2,$ $x_1+3x_2)$ ⇒ $A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3&1\\5&7\\1&3\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Rotation by $\varphi$ (counterclockwise): $\mathbf e_1\mapsto(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)$, $\mathbf e_2\mapsto(-\sin\varphi,\cos\varphi)$.
$A=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\varphi&-\sin\varphi\\ \sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{bmatrix}$; $\varphi=\tfrac\pi2$ gives $\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$.
Standard $2\times2$ maps (Tables 1–4). Reflect: $x_1$-axis $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, $x_2$-axis $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, line $x_2=x_1$ $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, line $x_2=-x_1$ $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, origin $-I$. Scale: $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}k&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ horiz., $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&k\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ vert. Shear: $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&k\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ horiz., $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\k&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ vert. Project: $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ onto $x_1$-axis, $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ onto $x_2$-axis.
Composition: track $\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2$ through each step. Shear $\mathbf e_2\mapsto\mathbf e_2-.5\mathbf e_1$, then reflect in the $x_2$-axis: $\mathbf e_1\mapsto\mathbf e_1\mapsto(-1,0)$, $\mathbf e_2\mapsto(-.5,1)\mapsto(.5,1)$ ⇒ $A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-1&.5\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Onto $\mathbb R^m$: every $\mathbf b$ is hit at least once (range = codomain; existence). One-to-one: every $\mathbf b$ is hit at most once (uniqueness).
$A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-4&8&1\\0&2&-1&3\\0&0&0&5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: pivot in every row ⇒ onto $\mathbb R^3$; a free variable ⇒ not one-to-one.
Thm 11Linear $T$ is one-to-one iff $T(\mathbf x)=\mathbf 0$ has only the trivial solution. Thm 12$T$ onto $\mathbb R^m$ ⇔ columns of $A$ span $\mathbb R^m$ (pivot every row); $T$ one-to-one ⇔ columns independent (pivot every column).
$A$ is $7\times5$ with 5 pivots: pivot in every column ⇒ one-to-one; only 5 of 7 rows ⇒ not onto. $A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3&1\\5&7\\1&3\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: one-to-one (columns not multiples), not onto $\mathbb R^3$ (only 2 columns).
WatchSize alone can kill it: onto needs $n\ge m$ (wide or square); one-to-one needs $m\ge n$ (tall or square); both ⇒ square with $n$ pivots.
Any $T:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^3$ is never onto; any $T:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^2$ is never one-to-one.

Matrix Operations (Ch. 2 restarts theorem numbers at 1)

KnowExample
Notation: $a_{ij}$ = row $i$, column $j$; main diagonal $a_{11},a_{22},\dots$; diagonal matrix = square, zero off the diagonal (e.g. $I_n$). $A+B$ and $rA$ are entrywise; $A+B$ needs the same size.
$\begin{bmatrix}4&0&5\\-1&3&2\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\3&5&7\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}5&1&6\\2&8&9\end{bmatrix}$
$A-2B=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&-2&3\\-7&-7&-12\end{smallmatrix}\right]$; $A+\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&-3\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ undefined.
Thm 1 · 2.1Sums/scalars behave normally: $A+B=B+A$, $(A+B)+C=A+(B+C)$, $A+0=A$, $r(A+B)=rA+rB$, $(r+s)A=rA+sA$, $r(sA)=(rs)A$.
So $A-2B$ can be computed directly, no need for $A+(-1)2B$.
Product (why it's defined this way): $AB$ is the matrix with $A(B\mathbf x)=(AB)\mathbf x$, i.e. matrix multiplication = composition of linear transformations. $AB=[\,A\mathbf b_1\ \cdots\ A\mathbf b_p\,]$ — each column of $AB$ is a combination of $A$'s columns weighted by that column of $B$.
$A=\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\1&-5\end{bmatrix},B=\begin{bmatrix}4&3&6\\1&-2&3\end{bmatrix}\Rightarrow AB=\begin{bmatrix}11&0&21\\-1&13&-9\end{bmatrix}$
first column $=A\mathbf b_1=4\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2\\1\end{smallmatrix}\right]+1\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3\\-5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Sizes: $(m\times n)(n\times p)=m\times p$ — inner numbers must match, outer numbers give the result.
$A$ is $3\times5$, $B$ is $5\times2$: $AB$ is $3\times2$; $BA$ undefined ($2\ne3$).
Row–column rule: $(AB)_{ij}=a_{i1}b_{1j}+\cdots+a_{in}b_{nj}$ (row $i$ of $A$ · column $j$ of $B$). One row at a time: $\operatorname{row}_i(AB)=\operatorname{row}_i(A)\cdot B$.
Same $A,B$: $(AB)_{13}=2(6)+3(3)=21$; $(AB)_{22}=1(3)+(-5)(-2)=13$.

Thm 13 · 4.5Basis Theorem: in a $p$-dim space ($p\ge1$), any $p$ independent vectors are a basis, and any $p$ spanning vectors are a basis. Right count ⇒ check only one condition.
Hermite $1,\ 2t,\ -2+4t^2,\ -12t+8t^3$: 4 independent (increasing degree) in 4-dim $\mathbb P_3$ ⇒ a basis.
rank $A=\dim\operatorname{Col}A$ = # pivot columns = $\dim\operatorname{Row}A$. nullity $A=\dim\operatorname{Nul}A$ = # free variables. Thm 14 · 4.5Rank Theorem: rank $A$ + nullity $A$ = $n$ (# columns).
$3\times5$ $A$ with 2 pivots: rank 2, nullity 3. $7\times9$ with nullity 2 ⇒ rank 7. $6\times9$ with nullity 2? Needs rank 7 > 6 — impossible.
IMT (continued), $n\times n$ $A$ invertible ⇔ (m) columns are a basis of $\mathbb R^n$ ⇔ (n) $\operatorname{Col}A=\mathbb R^n$ ⇔ (o) rank $A=n$ ⇔ (p) nullity $A=0$ ⇔ (q) $\operatorname{Nul}A=\{\mathbf 0\}$.
40 equations, 42 unknowns, 2-dim solution space ⇒ rank 40 ⇒ Col A $=\mathbb R^{40}$ ⇒ every $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ is solvable.
WatchRank theorem uses columns. rank $\le\min(m,n)$. Col A $\subseteq\mathbb R^m$, Nul A $\subseteq\mathbb R^n$: a 4×7 matrix with 4 pivots has Col A $=\mathbb R^4$ but Nul A is a 3-dim subspace of $\mathbb R^7$, not $\mathbb R^3$. A plane in $\mathbb R^3$ is 2-dim but not $\mathbb R^2$.
"Dependent subset of a $p$-dim space has more than $p$ vectors" — false ($\{\mathbf 0\}$). "$T$ bigger than a spanning set ⇒ $T$ dependent" — true.

5.1

Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues (Ch. 5 restarts theorem numbers at 1)

KnowExample
Eigenvector: nonzero $\mathbf x$ with $A\mathbf x=\lambda\mathbf x$ — a direction $A$ only stretches/flips, never turns. Eigenvalue $\lambda$ = the stretch factor. Eigenspace $=\operatorname{Nul}(A-\lambda I)$ (a subspace).
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&6\\5&2\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}6\\-5\end{smallmatrix}\right]=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-24\\20\end{smallmatrix}\right]=-4\left[\begin{smallmatrix}6\\-5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ ⇒ eigenvector, $\lambda=-4$.
Is $\mathbf x$ an eigenvector? Compute $A\mathbf x$; is it a multiple of $\mathbf x$? Is $\lambda$ an eigenvalue? Row reduce $A-\lambda I$ (subtract $\lambda$ on the diagonal): free variable ⇒ yes. Eigenspace basis: solve $(A-\lambda I)\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ in parametric form.
$\lambda=4$ for $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3&0&-1\\2&3&1\\-3&4&5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $A-4I\sim\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\\0&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ ⇒ yes, eigenvector $(-1,-1,1)$.
Thm 1 · 5.1Triangular ⇒ eigenvalues are the diagonal entries. 0 is an eigenvalue ⇔ $A$ not invertible. Thm 2 · 5.1Eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are independent.
$A\mathbf x=\lambda\mathbf x$ ⇒ $A^k\mathbf x=\lambda^k\mathbf x$, $A^{-1}\mathbf x=\lambda^{-1}\mathbf x$. If $A^2=0$ the only eigenvalue is 0. $\mathbf x_k=\lambda^k\mathbf x_0$ solves $\mathbf x_{k+1}=A\mathbf x_k$.
WatchEigenvectors are nonzero (eigenvalues may be 0). Row reducing $A$ does not reveal eigenvalues. $2\mathbf v$ has the same eigenvalue as $\mathbf v$. Independent eigenvectors needn't have distinct eigenvalues.
Only triangular matrices have eigenvalues on the diagonal.
5.2

The Characteristic Equation

KnowExample
Eigenvalues = roots of $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$ (the characteristic equation; $\det(A-\lambda I)$ = characteristic polynomial, degree $n$). Multiplicity = how many times a root repeats. $2\times2$: $\lambda^2-(\operatorname{tr}A)\lambda+\det A$.
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&3\\3&-6\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $(2-\lambda)(-6-\lambda)-9=\lambda^2+4\lambda-21=(\lambda-3)(\lambda+7)$ ⇒ $3,-7$.
$3\times3$: expand $\det(A-\lambda I)$ along a row/column with zeros, keep it factored. No real roots ⇒ no real eigenvalues.
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}4&0&0\\5&3&2\\-2&0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: row 1 ⇒ $(4-\lambda)(3-\lambda)(2-\lambda)$. $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}5&3\\-4&4\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $\lambda^2-9\lambda+32$, no real roots.
IMT (r): invertible ⇔ 0 not an eigenvalue. $\det A$ = product of eigenvalues. $A$ and $A^T$: same eigenvalues. Eigenspace dim ≤ multiplicity.
Make the $\lambda=5$ eigenspace 2-D: need 2 free variables in $A-5I$ ⇒ solve for the parameter (Ex. 18: $h=6$).
Similar: $B=P^{-1}AP$. Thm 4 · 5.2Similar ⇒ same characteristic polynomial ⇒ same eigenvalues (with multiplicity).
WatchSame eigenvalues ⇏ similar ($\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ vs $2I$). Similar ≠ row equivalent. $(\lambda+5)$ factor ⇒ eigenvalue $-5$.
5.3

Diagonalization

KnowExample
Diagonalizable: $A=PDP^{-1}$, $D$ diagonal. Meaning: in eigenvector coordinates $A$ just scales each axis. Payoff: $A^k=PD^kP^{-1}$ ($D^k$ = powers of the diagonal).
$P=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}5&7\\2&3\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$: $A^4=PD^4P^{-1}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}226&-525\\90&-209\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Thm 5 · 5.3Diagonalizable ⇔ $n$ independent eigenvectors. $P$ = eigenvectors as columns, $D$ = matching eigenvalues in the same order. Check with $AP=PD$.
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\6&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $\lambda=1\to(1,3)$, $\lambda=-1\to(0,1)$ ⇒ $P=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\3&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$.
Thm 6 · 5.3$n$ distinct eigenvalues ⇒ diagonalizable. Thm 7 · 5.3Repeated eigenvalue: diagonalizable ⇔ eigenspace dims add to $n$ (each dim = its multiplicity).
$\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3&-1\\1&5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: $(\lambda-4)^2$, but $A-4I$ has 1 free variable ⇒ only 1 eigenvector ⇒ not diagonalizable.
WatchNeed $n$ independent eigenvectors, not $n$ eigenvalues. Diagonalizable and invertible are unrelated. Diagonalizable ⇏ distinct eigenvalues. $D$ must be diagonal.
$5\times5$ with eigenspaces of dim 3 and 2 ⇒ yes. $3\times3$ with two 1-D eigenspaces ⇒ no.
5.4

Eigenvectors and Linear Transformations

KnowExample
$\mathcal B$-matrix of $T:V\to V$: columns $[T(\mathbf b_j)]_{\mathcal B}$ (images of the basis vectors, in $\mathcal B$-coordinates). Then $[T(\mathbf x)]_{\mathcal B}=[T]_{\mathcal B}[\mathbf x]_{\mathcal B}$. Same map, written in a different basis.
$T(\mathbf b_1)=3\mathbf b_1-5\mathbf b_2$, $T(\mathbf b_2)=-\mathbf b_1+6\mathbf b_2$, $T(\mathbf b_3)=4\mathbf b_2$ ⇒ $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3&-1&0\\-5&6&4\\0&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ (columns).
Polynomials: apply $T$ to $1,t,t^2$ one at a time; coefficient lists are the columns. Eigenvector of $T$: $T(\mathbf p)=\lambda\mathbf p$.
$T(a_0+a_1t+a_2t^2)=3a_0+(5a_0-2a_1)t+(4a_1+a_2)t^2$ ⇒ $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}3&0&0\\5&-2&0\\0&4&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
$T(\mathbf x)=A\mathbf x$ on $\mathbb R^n$: $[T]_{\mathcal B}=P^{-1}AP$, $P=[\,\mathbf b_1\cdots\mathbf b_n\,]$. Thm 8 · 5.4If $A=PDP^{-1}$, the columns of $P$ form the basis where $T$'s matrix is the diagonal $D$.
Diagonal basis for $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-3&4\end{smallmatrix}\right]$: eigenvectors $(1,1)$ ($\lambda=1$), $(1,3)$ ($\lambda=3$) ⇒ $[T]_{\mathcal B}=\operatorname{diag}(1,3)$.
WatchSimilar ⇒ same eigenvalues, not same eigenvectors. $P^{-1}AP$, not $PAP^{-1}$. Nonzero kernel vector ⇒ 0 is an eigenvalue.
Similar matrices share rank, det, trace, invertibility, diagonalizability.
map

Master dictionary ($A$ is $m\times n$, $T(\mathbf x)=A\mathbf x$)

Existence · pivot in every row

  • $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ consistent for every $\mathbf b\in\mathbb R^m$
  • columns span $\mathbb R^m$; every $\mathbf b$ is a combination of them
  • $T$ is onto $\mathbb R^m$; range = codomain
  • needs $m$ pivots ⇒ $n\ge m$

Uniqueness · pivot in every column

  • $A\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$ has only the trivial solution; no free variables
  • columns linearly independent
  • $T$ is one-to-one; each $\mathbf b$ has ≤ 1 preimage
  • needs $n$ pivots ⇒ $m\ge n$

If $A$ is square ($m=n$), the two columns collapse into one — that is the Invertible Matrix Theorem: $A$ invertible ⇔ $A\sim I_n$ ⇔ $n$ pivots ⇔ columns independent ⇔ columns span $\mathbb R^n$ ⇔ one-to-one ⇔ onto ⇔ $A^{T}$ invertible ⇔ $\det A\ne0$, and then $\mathbf x=A^{-1}\mathbf b$. For non-square $A$ they stay independent.

One specific $\mathbf b$: $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ consistent ⇔ $\mathbf b\in$ Span(columns) ⇔ $\mathbf b$ in range of $T$ ⇔ last column of $[A\ \mathbf b]$ not a pivot column. Solutions $=\mathbf p+$ (solutions of $A\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$).

Counting: #pivots $\le\min(m,n)$; free variables $=n-$#pivots; $p>n$ vectors in $\mathbb R^n$ ⇒ dependent; homogeneous with more unknowns than equations ⇒ nontrivial solutions.
